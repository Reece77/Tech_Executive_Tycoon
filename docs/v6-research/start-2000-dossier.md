## The bust and the eras (formulas §60.5)

- **NASDAQ:** peak 10 March 2000 (5,048.62), trough 9 October 2002 (1,114.11), a 78% fall (S: Wikipedia, "Dot-com bubble"). The game has no index series of its own before 2010: the bust reaches capital markets through the venture climate (round sizes, ARR multiples and IPO book demand) and the shut IPO window (July 2000 to December 2003; R: Jay Ritter's IPO counts, 406 in 2000, 80 in 2001, 66 in 2002, 63 in 2003, 173 in 2004).
- **Venture funding by year** (R: PwC/NVCA MoneyTree): about $100B in 2000 (the peak, Q1 2000), $40B in 2001, $21B in 2002, $19B in 2003 (the trough), then $22–30B a year to 2007.
- **History eras:** Facebook (4 February 2004), Amazon S3 (14 March 2006), Netflix streaming (16 January 2007), the iPhone (29 June 2007) (S: Wikipedia); the Tesla Roadster (production March 2008) and Spotify (7 October 2008) are held to January 2008, the 2008 world's first month.
- **Regulation:** *US v. Microsoft* (Jackson's order June 2000, reversed June 2001, settled November 2001), Sarbanes-Oxley (July 2002, not modelled: the game has no listing-cost rule), the EU's Microsoft decision (March 2004, EUR 497M).
