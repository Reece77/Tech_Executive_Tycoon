# M23a: v3 integration and accounting (lane A)

**Goal:** make v3's systems work together as one group in one world. Fabs, mines and refineries supply any company in the player's group at transfer price; rivals fall under the same export controls; facilities and suppliers sit in real countries; contracts can be terminated, and foundry capacity can be sold under contract. Fix Codex review 5's twelve findings, most of which are the same gap: one company owns an asset while its group uses it.

**ADRs:** 0002, 0003, 0004, 0007, 0013, 0020 · **Formulas:** §31–§39 · **Review:** `docs/reviews/codex-review-5.md` · **Playtest:** `docs/playtests/reece-playtest-5.md` · **Roadmap:** `docs/roadmap.md` (Phase 1) · **Depends on:** M22 (`m22-done`) · **Lane:** A, alongside M23c (lane C) and M23b (lane B)

## Sub-tasks (commit after each)

1. **A shared group-ownership layer** (findings 1, 4, 5, 7, and M21's follow-up): one function moves a facility between group companies and updates every kind-specific record (mine, refinery, fab), its staffing and its books; one routing rule sends a subsidiary's critical events to the player's inbox, naming the affected company and facility; every facility kind can **supply any group company** at the flow's transfer price (fabs to a sister company's chips, M20's wafers into the group's fabs, mines and refineries to any group buyer); materials flows feed the minority-fairness monitor and the intra-group share. **Tests:** Codex's reproductions for findings 1, 4, 5 and 7; a three-company chain (mine → refinery → fab → device company) supplying at transfer price, with every posting on the right entity.
2. **Mines, fabs and accounting** (findings 2, 3, 6): depletion and capitalized exploration tracked per deposit across mine lifetimes (no rebuild with restored reserves); NCI attribution computed before intercompany elimination; fixed fab costs follow the facility's lifecycle, not its staffing. Regression tests as the findings ask.
3. **Money, identity, hidden values and tests** (findings 8, 9, 10, 11, 12): stable keys for entity-list and appeal draws; public planning estimates (or no economics) in fab quotes for unarrived nodes, with a differential test; offtake prepayments and every new v3 money path through `money.exact` (an audit of mining, refining, fab and tariff money), with rational-reference tests; the founder's salary monthly; the tariff property test made meaningful (non-empty taxed cases, independent expected amounts, own-fab and mixed-origin cases).
4. **Rivals under export controls** (M22 follow-up; playtest 5): rival chip and equipment companies in the controlling bloc lose sales to restricted destinations as the player does (NVIDIA's China revenue after October 2022, with compliant variants where history had them: A800, H20); Chinese rivals gain share where they can supply. Rival foundries and suppliers obey tool and input controls. **Test:** a software-only company still plays exactly as before; §39.4's targets re-run with rivals controlled.
5. **Countries everywhere** (M22 follow-up; playtest 5): a country choice for new facilities and subsidiaries (no silent default to China for East Asia); suppliers carry a country (Dowa: Japan) shown wherever a supplier is listed; tariffs carried in transfer prices between group companies.
6. **Contracts** (playtest 5):
   - **Terminate** on every contract and offtake: a fee of the remaining months' take-or-pay minimum, discounted at the base rate, plus `contracts.break_penalty` (10%), quoted before confirming; the counterparty's willingness to deal falls (a supplier relationship score that later offers read).
   - **Foundry contracts** (playtest 5, fab card): a fab's owner can sign multi-month slot contracts with rival fabless designers at a price and volume, with take-or-pay on the buyer, instead of selling only spot slots by chance.
   - **Offtakes protect production:** volume under a group's offtakes or own supply keeps 100% allocation in shortages (M19 follow-up 3; playtest 5's "I lost production").
7. **Close-out:** a Resolution section in `docs/reviews/codex-review-5.md`; CHANGELOG; data dictionary; golden snapshots regenerated only if a fix moves them (reason in CHANGELOG); the nightly guards once.

## Decisions made

1. **A facility's crew moves with it** (finding 1). `staff_of` counts only the facility owner's employees, so a transfer that left the crew behind would leave the plant unstaffed with its fixed costs running. `move_facility` moves a unit of the old owner that staffs only this facility, with its active people (their pay follows on the new owner's payroll); a unit that staffs anything else stays, and the facility is left for the new owner to staff.
2. **One routing rule for the group's inbox** (finding 4): every item about a company of the player's group goes to the player's inbox (`group.assets.inbox_company`): refinery incidents, curbs and contract ends, fab notices. At most one refinery incident a month across the group (the old rule was one a company; all now share one inbox). The incident's inbox item names the plant and its owner (`{owner}`); the event card's text is unchanged (its `{company}` is the player's).
3. **Sister-company fab supply is priced by the `chips` flow** (cost by default: the fab's cost a start this month; cost plus or the node's market wafer price if the holding sets it). The fab's owner books the whole month's costs; the buyer pays the transfer price (5900/4300). The M16 abstract `chips` flow still applies to the buyer's bought chips; the fairness monitor adds the real fab supply to it.
4. **Wafer plants feed fabs on last month's run** (refineries run in phase 4, fabs settle in phase 5): a group fab wants its previous month's starts in substrate, after the chip products' wafer starts.
5. **A month with no materials moving leaves a materials flow's record as it was** (finding 7): neither the unfair months nor a standing complaint reset because nothing traded.
6. **A rebuild after a chosen closure is allowed** and inherits the deposit (finding 2). Refusing it on hidden reserves would reveal them; a deposit whose mine closed as depleted, or that was sold or seized, cannot be rebuilt.
7. **Fixed fab costs on live capex parts** (finding 6): fab.fixed_share x the capex that has gone live (the build, then each conversion when it goes live), for as long as the facility is live. Offline while converting, the fab keeps paying on the old capex.
8. **Unarrived nodes' quotes use public planning economics** (finding 9): the previous node's planning capital and wafer price x the middle of fab.future_capital_mult [1.08, 1.17] and fab.future_wafer_mult [1.2, 1.5]. A preorder is still built at the real (hidden) capital: the difference stays hidden like the overrun.
9. **Own-fab chips are taxed at their full cost a good die** (finding 5), not the packaging they post at sale; a sister company's fab chips are taxed at the border into the buyer's country on the transfer price (`border_tariff`) and then count as made there.
10. **Rivals under controls** (sub-task 4): rivals request no licences; they sell to a destination only through a variant from history (A800 from November 2022, voided by October 2023's rule; H20 from January 2024, voided by April 2025's) or a quota. The local-partner rule stays the player's (rivals' China operations are abstract). Demand a blocked product cannot win goes to the region's free products by choice share, without their seller capacity cap. `geopolitics.rivals_controlled` (true) switches this off for comparison runs.
11. **A Chinese accelerator rival from history**: Huawei Ascend (October 2018, East Asia, cost leader, 60 staff, $2B, launched at quality 55, A). Rival chip startups never reached the category's launch quality 30 (Cerebras and Groq stay in development), so an entrant may now start with a launched product (`entrants.yaml` `quality`) and in another region (`region`).
12. **Rival foundries' tool controls stay as SMIC's static `euv_gate`** (nodes.yaml): its reach stops at 7 nm by DUV while the EUV controls hold; a d�tente lifting them is a follow-up. Suppliers obey input controls: a supplier signs no new offtake into its own country's biting ban (Chalco's gallium to the US from December 2024; Dowa, Japan, still can).
13. **A country choice is optional in the engine**: with none, a subsidiary, fab or refinery sits in its city's or region's default country as before (balance scripts and old saves); the API takes `country`, and the choice is recorded as a 39.1 site. The country-choice controls on the screens are M23c's.
14. **Termination fee** = the months the contract still covers (this one included) x the month's take-or-pay minimum, discounted monthly at the base rate from next month, x 1.10, in exact fractions; to 6900. An offtake's unused prepayment counts against it. Mine sales had no take-or-pay: `contracts.mine_take_or_pay` 0.30 (as 33.3's contracts) prices their break. Counterparties: -0.30 a break, +0.01 a month, new deals up to 25% dearer (or cheaper to sell to), none below 0.25.
15. **Foundry contracts**: rival designers' demand is abstract, so they take every contracted start the fab delivers; a fab short of starts owes nothing; `contracts.foundry.take_or_pay` (0.3) prices the owner's early termination. Price at most the node's slot price; one designer at most 40% of capacity; at most 90% under contract.
16. **Every offtake is protected volume** in a shortage, prepaid or not (playtest 5's "I lost production"); fixed-price contracts keep M12a's before-the-shortage rule. A group mine's last month's sales to its group buyer are protected volume too (M19 follow-up 3).
17. **New records without a new table**: `relations` and `foundry_contracts` are `geo_record` kinds (appended, so earlier rows keep their numbers); new fields ride in existing JSON. Migration 0048 adds only `production_month.fab_chips`.
