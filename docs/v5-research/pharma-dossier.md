## Attrition and timelines

- **Phase transition rates:** BIO, Informa and QLS, *Clinical Development Success Rates 2011–2020* (2021): Phase I→II about 52%, II→III about 29%, III→submission about 58%, submission→approval about 91%; likelihood of approval from Phase I about 7.9%; by area, haematology highest (about 24%) and oncology lowest (about 5%) *(verify)*. Cross-check: Wong, Siah and Lo, *Biostatistics* 2019 *(verify)*.
- **Durations:** about 10–15 years from discovery to approval; Phase I to approval about 10.5 years on average (BIO 2021) *(verify)*.
- **Costs:** DiMasi et al. 2016, about $2.6B per approved drug including the cost of capital and failures; Wouters et al., *JAMA* 2020, median about $985M and mean about $1.3B *(verify)*; trial cost per patient by phase and area *(verify)*.

## Exclusivity and the cliff

- US patents 20 years from filing, with term restoration up to 5 years (Hatch-Waxman, 1984); data exclusivity 5 years (new chemical entity) and 12 years (biologics, BPCIA 2010); orphan exclusivity 7 years; EU 8+2+1 and orphan 10 *(verify)*.
- Generic erosion: prices fall about 80–90% within a year with several generic entrants (FDA analyses) *(verify)*; biosimilar uptake slower (Humira's US biosimilars from 2023) *(verify)*.

## Pricing and rules

- US Inflation Reduction Act (2022): Medicare price negotiation, the first ten drugs' negotiated prices effective January 2026; further rounds dated *(verify)*. EU and UK HTA (NICE) *(verify)*.
- FDA expedited programmes: priority review (2017+ figures), accelerated approval, breakthrough therapy (2012) *(verify)*.

## AI-driven discovery

- AlphaFold 2 (2020–2021); Insilico Medicine's first AI-discovered molecule into Phase II (2023); Isomorphic Labs (2021) and partnerships (2024); evidence on AI's effect on Phase I success rates (for example Jayatunga et al. 2024, *Drug Discovery Today*: higher Phase I success for AI-discovered molecules) *(verify)*.
