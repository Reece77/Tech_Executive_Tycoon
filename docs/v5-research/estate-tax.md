## Europe: United Kingdom inheritance tax (with Germany, France)

- Rate 40% above the nil-rate band (NRB); 36% if 10%+ of the net estate goes to charity (from April 2012) (from memory, unverified).
- NRB £325,000 unchanged since April 2009 and frozen to at least April 2030 (Autumn Budget 2025 extended to April 2031).
  https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/inheritance-tax-nil-rate-band-and-residence-nil-rate-bands-from-6-april-2028
- Residence NRB (main home to descendants): £100,000 (2017/18), £125,000 (2018/19), £150,000 (2019/20), £175,000 (2020/21 on);
  tapered away £1 per £2 of estate above £2M, so gone at £2.35M (one person). Unused NRB/RNRB transfers to a spouse.
  https://www.hcrlaw.com/news-and-insights/the-silent-tax-rise-how-frozen-nil-rate-bands-and-surging-property-values-have-created-a-hidden-inheritance-tax-burden/
- Gifts: no separate lifetime allowance. Outright gifts are potentially exempt transfers, tax-free if the donor survives 7 years;
  taper relief reduces tax for deaths in years 3-7; £3,000 annual exemption. https://www.gov.uk/inheritance-tax/gifts
- Business Property Relief: 100% on unlisted trading-company shares (key for founders). From 6 April 2026 the 100% rate is capped
  at a combined BPR/APR allowance (announced £1M, raised to £2.5M in Dec 2025), 50% above; AIM shares 50% (from memory, unverified).
- Trusts: limited shelter. Lifetime transfers into most trusts are charged 20% above the NRB, plus up to 6% every 10 years.
- Germany (Erbschaftsteuer): class I (spouse, children) 7-30%, class III up to 50%; allowances €500k spouse, €400k per child
  (since 2009); gifts aggregated over 10 years; business assets 85% or 100% relief (reformed 2016) (from memory, unverified).
- France (droits de succession): direct line 5-45%; €100,000 per child per parent (since Aug 2012, was €159,325); gifts recalled
  over 15 years; Pacte Dutreil exempts 75% of family-business value (from memory, unverified).

## East Asia: Japan (相続税 inheritance tax, NTA)

| Period | Basic deduction | Top rate |
|---|---|---|
| 2010-2014 | ¥50M + ¥10M per statutory heir | 50% (above ¥300M per heir share) |
| 2015-2025 | ¥30M + ¥6M per statutory heir | 55% (above ¥600M per heir share), 8 brackets from 10% |

Example, spouse + 2 children (3 heirs): ¥80M (~$909k) in 2010; ¥48M (~$320k) in 2025. 2015 reform enacted in the FY2013 tax reform.
Sources: https://www.nta.go.jp/english/taxes/individual/12012.htm ,
https://www.bakermckenzie.co.jp/wp/wp-content/uploads/ClientAlert_201501_Tax_E.pdf , https://www.nippon.com/en/currents/d00154
- Gifts: separate gift tax, ¥1.1M annual exemption, top 55%. Gifts made within 3 years of death (7 years from 2024) are pulled back
  into the estate; optional "settlement at inheritance" system (¥25M) unifies gifts and inheritance (from memory, unverified).
- Business succession tax system (事業承継税制) defers tax on unlisted company shares; special 100% deferral for plans filed 2018-2027.
- Trusts: weak shelter; trust beneficiaries are taxed as if they owned the assets. Spouse deduction covers the greater of ¥160M
  or the statutory share. Adoption of grandchildren to add heirs is a known tactic.

## South and Southeast Asia: India

- Estate duty abolished for deaths on or after 16 March 1985; Gift Tax Act repealed from 1 October 1998 (from memory, unverified).
- Since 2004 (now s.56(2)(x) Income-tax Act), gifts above ₹50,000 a year from non-relatives are taxed as the recipient's income;
  gifts from relatives and inheritances are exempt. https://incometaxindia.gov.in/Pages/i-am/individual.aspx
- Trusts: private family trusts are common for holding promoter stakes (e.g. Bajaj, Godrej, Tata trusts) to keep control together.

## Latin America: Brazil (ITCMD, state-level)

- ITCMD is levied by each state on inheritances and gifts (doações) alike. Federal Senate Resolution 9/1992 caps the rate at 8%.
- São Paulo: flat 4% (2010 and 2025). Several states (e.g. Bahia, Santa Catarina) run progressive scales up to 8%.
- Constitutional Amendment 132/2023 makes progressive rates mandatory and taxes the state of the deceased's domicile;
  complementary law LC 227/2026 implements this (details still unsettled). Proposals to raise the cap (up to 16%) pending.
  https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/constituicao/emendas/emc/emc132.htm ,
  https://jus.com.br/artigos/112364/a-obrigatoriedade-das-novas-aliquotas-trazidas-pela-reforma-tributaria-brasileira-ao-imposto-de-transmissao-causa-mortis-e-doacao-no-instituto-da-heranca-e-da-doacao
- Exemptions are small, state-specific thresholds (from memory, unverified); the game treats it as zero.
- Trusts: not recognised in Brazilian civil law. Family holding companies ("holding familiar") with gifted quotas and usufruct
  reserved to the founder are the usual vehicle. Law 14.754/2023 taxes income of offshore trusts.

## Africa: South Africa (estate duty, SARS)

- Rate 20% on the dutiable estate; from 1 March 2018, 25% on the part above R30M.
- Abatement R3,500,000 (s.4A, since March 2007), unchanged 2010-2025; unused amount ports to a surviving spouse (up to R7M);
  bequests to a spouse are deductible. R3.5M is ~$479k (2010), ~$191k (2025).
  https://www.sars.gov.za/types-of-tax/estate-duty/ , https://www.sars.gov.za/lapd-it-g31-faqs-on-deceased-estates
- Gifts: donations tax 20% (25% above R30M cumulative from 2018), R100,000 annual exemption, gifts to spouse exempt.
  https://www.sars.gov.za/types-of-tax/donations-tax/
- Trusts: widely used to freeze estate value, but s.7C (2017) treats low-interest loans to trusts as annual donations.

## Oceania: Australia

- No inheritance or estate tax: federal estate duty abolished for deaths after 1 July 1979; states abolished theirs 1977-1982
  (from memory, unverified). No gift tax.
- Death is not a CGT event: beneficiaries inherit the deceased's cost base and pay CGT only on sale.
  https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/capital-gains-tax/inherited-assets-and-capital-gains-tax
- Trusts: discretionary family trusts are the standard vehicle for holding private-company shares.

## Succession history for founder-led companies

| Company | One-line summary |
|---|---|
| Walmart (Walton family) | Family holds ~45% through Walton Enterprises and trusts; Audrey Walton's GRATs won *Walton v. Commissioner* (2000), making zeroed-out GRATs a standard tool. |
| Ford | 1956 IPO happened because the Ford Foundation, given non-voting shares to dodge estate tax after Edsel (1943) and Henry (1947) died, needed to sell; Class B shares still give the family ~40% of votes on ~2% of equity. |
| Samsung (Lee family) | After Lee Kun-hee died (Oct 2020) heirs declared ~12 trillion won (~$10.8B) of inheritance tax in April 2021, paid in six instalments over five years funded by stock loans and share sales. https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/amp/business/companies/20230606/samsung-family-takes-out-stock-loans-to-pay-inheritance-tax |
| Estée Lauder | Family keeps control through Class B 10-vote shares after the 1995 IPO, which was partly an estate-tax liquidity event (from memory, unverified). |
| News Corp / Fox (Murdoch) | The irrevocable Murdoch Family Trust holds ~40% of voting stock; after a 2024 Nevada probate fight, a Sept 2025 deal bought out three siblings (~$3.3B) to keep Lachlan in control (from memory, unverified). |
| Hermès | Family pooled ~50%+ into holding company H51 (2011) with pre-emption rights to block LVMH's creeping stake (from memory, unverified). |
| Microsoft (Gates) | Most of Bill Gates's fortune goes to the Gates Foundation (charitable transfers are estate-tax exempt); in 2025 he pledged to give away nearly all of it by 2045, when the foundation closes. |

Pattern for the game: high estate tax plus illiquid founder equity forces share sales or pledged-share loans; trusts, dual-class
shares and family holding companies keep voting control even when economic ownership is diluted.
