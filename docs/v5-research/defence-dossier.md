- **Budgets:** SIPRI military expenditure by country and year, 2010–2024 (world about $2.7 trillion in 2024; US about $997 billion) *(verify)*; US federal IT spending (about $100 billion a year) *(verify)*.
- **Cloud:** JEDI (awarded to Microsoft in 2019, protested by AWS, cancelled July 2021); JWCC (December 2022, up to $9 billion, shared by AWS, Google, Microsoft and Oracle) *(verify)*.
- **AI:** Project Maven (2017; Google's withdrawal after staff protests, 2018); the CDAO (2022) *(verify)*.
- **Space:** NSSL Phase 2 (2020: ULA and SpaceX) and Phase 3 (2024–2025); the Space Development Agency's tranches *(verify)*.
- **Chips:** the Trusted Foundry Program; CHIPS Act (2022) defence provisions; the Secure Enclave programme (2024) *(verify)*.
- **Sourcing rules:** Section 889 of the 2019 NDAA (Huawei, ZTE and others; part B from August 2020); DFARS domestic-content rules; ITAR *(verify)*.
- **Clearances:** median investigation times by level (DCSA reports) *(verify)*.
- **Contract types:** fixed-price, cost-plus, IDIQ; GAO bid-protest statistics (sustain and effectiveness rates) *(verify)*.

## Verification (M44, 2026-11-19)

The M44 session checked every *(verify)* item above and built `backend/data/government.yaml` from the results; each value there carries a tag (S sourced, D derived, R recalled or read from a secondary report, A an assumption with its basis) and a key of the file's `sources` table, and the loader refuses a value without one. The session's network could not open sipri.org, gao.gov, data.sec.gov or sgp.fas.org directly, so figures were read from SIPRI's own Milex workbook (a public mirror of the April 2025 release), from en.wikipedia.org pages citing primary sources, and from search results quoting the sources. Corrections and readings:

- **Budgets.** SIPRI's database revises every year: its current-dollar US figure for 2010 is $738.0 billion, where the 2011 fact sheet printed $698 billion; the game uses the April 2025 database throughout (one consistent series). 2024: world $2,718 billion, US $997.3 billion (37%), China about $314 billion, Russia about $149 billion, Germany $88.5 billion. SIPRI publishes no EU-27 total: the game's "other EU governments" is the member states' sum less France and Germany (D). US federal IT: about $78 billion in FY2010 (sources disagree, $76-82 billion; R), about $80 billion in 2015, over $90 billion in 2020, about $95 billion on the IT Dashboard in FY2024 (S, GAO), which leaves out about $37 billion of DoD national-security IT.
- **Cloud.** JEDI: awarded to Microsoft on 25 October 2019 ($10 billion ceiling, up to 10 years); AWS protested in November 2019 (the exact filing dates are R); cancelled on 6 July 2021, with JWCC announced the same day. JWCC: 7 December 2022, AWS, Google, Microsoft and Oracle on a shared $9 billion ceiling, ordering to about June 2028 (S).
- **AI.** Project Maven: the Work memo of 26 April 2017; about 3,100 signatures on the April 2018 letter (about 4,000 by May), about a dozen resignations, non-renewal announced 1 June 2018 (S). The CDAO: memo 8 December 2021, initial operating capability 1 February 2022, full on 1 June 2022 (the dossier's "initial operating June 2022" is the full capability).
- **Space.** NSSL Phase 2: 7 August 2020, ULA $337 million and SpaceX $316 million of first orders; the 60/40 split is of about 34 missions, not dollars. Phase 3 Lane 1: 13 June 2024, $5.6 billion ceiling (later raised). Lane 2: 4 April 2025, about $13.7 billion (SpaceX about $5.9 billion, ULA about $5.4 billion, Blue Origin about $2.4 billion). SDA Tranche 1 Transport: 28 February 2022, about $1.8 billion (Lockheed, Northrop, York); Tranche 2 Tracking: January 2024, about $2.5 billion (L3Harris, Lockheed, Sierra Space). Tranche 2 Transport Beta's split is unverified.
- **Chips.** Trusted Foundry: from 2004 with IBM (GAO-16-185T); the "IBM $600 million, 10 years" figure could not be confirmed and is not used. CHIPS and Science Act: signed 9 August 2022, $52.7 billion for semiconductors. Secure Enclave: Intel, up to $3 billion, 16 September 2024 (S).
- **Sourcing rules.** Section 889 (Pub. L. 115-232): part A from 13 August 2019, part B from 13 August 2020; Huawei, ZTE, Hytera, Hikvision and Dahua (S). Buy American (FAR, 7 March 2022): domestic content 60% from 25 October 2022, 65% in 2024, 75% in 2029 (S); 55% from January 2020 and 50% before (R). ITAR: 22 CFR 120-130, DDTC; 126.1's proscribed destinations include China and Russia.
- **Clearances.** Fastest 90% of initial cases, end to end: Top Secret 534-543 days and Secret 221-259 days in early FY2018, with a backlog of about 725,000 in April 2018; about 249 and 138 days in Q4 FY2024 (S, PAC via ClearanceJobs). Denials: 0.2% of DCSA's FY2021 adjudications, 6.5-9.2% of initial cases at CIA and NSA in FY2014 (S). The game's "never clear" share (5% Secret, 8% Top Secret) adds discontinued cases to denials (A); 57.6 target 3 needs at least 5%.
- **Protests (GAO).** FY2010: 19% sustained (S), about 2,299 cases and a 42% effectiveness rate (R); FY2015: 2,639 cases, 12%, 45%; FY2020: 2,149, 15%, 51%; FY2023: 2,025, 31% (an outlier), 57%; FY2024: 1,803, 16%, 52% (S).
- **Primes.** Lockheed Martin $45.67 billion (2010) and $71.04 billion (2024); General Dynamics $32.47 billion (2010); L3Harris $21.3 billion (2024); Palantir $2.87 billion (2024) (S). RTX, Northrop, Boeing Defense, BAE and L-3's figures, and Palantir's 2020 revenue, are recalled (R). Anduril: about $1 billion in 2024 (company, via TechCrunch; R). Primes' technical merit and past performance by area are game assumptions ranked from SIPRI's Top 100 (A).
